# Privacy as Method

## Public archive rule

**Publish the history, not the keys to a person.**

The Anonymous Family is deliberately narrower in public than it is in private. That difference is part of the research design.

The private working archive may contain names, exact relationships, source files, working trees, contributed material, and research notes. The public artifact is designed to preserve historical meaning while withholding identifying connective tissue around living people.

## What the public layer may contain

The public edition may publish:

- aggregate generation counts
- regions and migration currents
- eras and occupational patterns
- evidence classifications
- descriptions of archival gaps and uncertainty
- already-public historical records involving deceased people when a name is necessary to understand the evidence
- redacted or transformed material that cannot reasonably identify living relatives

## What the public layer does not publish

The public repository and exhibit must not contain:

1. living-person lineage nodes
2. exact living-person birth or other identifying dates
3. private addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, or contact details
4. raw GEDCOM files
5. private genealogy exports or database dumps
6. sensitive record identifiers
7. unredacted personal documents
8. direct public chains from the project steward to named living relatives
9. hidden copies of the above in source folders, backups, comments, build artifacts, or commit-ready files

## Evidence is not flattened

The exhibit distinguishes among:

- **Document-confirmed** — original or official evidence directly supports the claim.
- **Family-confirmed** — known within the family and directly confirmed by the steward or close family.
- **Working tree lead** — present in a GEDCOM or contributed tree and still requiring generation-by-generation review.
- **Family tradition** — meaningful inherited information that may guide research but is not treated as verified merely because it has been repeated.

## Stewardship credit

Brad Devowe is publicly credited as project steward by consent. That credit must not be used as the first node in a public chain that identifies other living relatives.

## Before publishing a new edition

Before any new file, image, scan, dataset, map, or interactive element is made public:

- check whether it identifies a living person directly
- check whether several harmless-looking details can be combined to identify someone
- strip private metadata when appropriate
- redact or aggregate before publication
- preserve uncertainty instead of converting a working lead into a fact
- keep the private research archive outside this public repository

If a new element cannot preserve the public/private boundary, it belongs in the private research archive rather than this repository.
